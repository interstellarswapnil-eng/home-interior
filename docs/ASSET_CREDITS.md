# Asset credits

All third-party assets are **CC0 (public domain)** from [Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com/license). No attribution is legally required, but the authors are listed below. Everything is downloaded and processed by `npm run fetch-assets` (`scripts/fetch-assets.ts`), which also regenerates `public/ASSET_SOURCES.generated.md`.

**Processing:**
- **Albedo recolouring.** Albedo maps are recoloured to the `plan/materials.ts` palette with a luminance-preserving remap. The texture keeps its real grout, grain and weave, but lands on the lighter-modern colours. Normal and roughness maps are the originals.
- **Resolution.** Every map is 1k (1024²) JPG, and the HDRI is 1k. That's about 11 MB in total.
- **Granite.** The pale artificial granite is generated procedurally: seamless speckle, soft mottling and faint veins. The CC0 granite scans are laid as tiles with grout lines, which is wrong for a continuous countertop.
- **Side-table model.** `side_table_01` keeps its geometry but is re-skinned in the project's light-oak laminate, so it matches the palette.

## Furniture models

The rest of the furniture is **parametric, built in code** (`components/Furniture3D.tsx`) to the exact `plan/plan.ts` footprints. That covers the sofa, lounge chairs, coffee table, beds, desk, chair, wardrobes, kitchen, sanitaryware and curtains. No third-party assets are involved.

The whole Poly Haven CC0 furniture catalogue was reviewed first. Almost all of it is ornate, antique, leather or dark wood, for example `Sofa_01`, `sofa_02`, `ArmChair_01`, `GothicBed_01` and `CoffeeTable_01`. It would contradict the lighter-modern brief. There are no suitable beds, so only the light-oak side table was usable.
