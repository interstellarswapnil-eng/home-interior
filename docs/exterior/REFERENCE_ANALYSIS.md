# Reference analysis

There are 29 images in `exterior_references/`, numbered here in filename order (see the index at the end).

**About the colours.**
- The **sampled** hex values come from the image pixels (k-means, 7 clusters, centre crop), so they are real measurements.
- About two-thirds of the images are dusk renders with warm lights on, which makes walls read darker and more orange than the actual paint. Where an image is a dusk shot, I also give a **paint estimate**: the colour the wall would be in daylight.
- Coverage percentages are my eyeball estimates of the facade only (not sky or road).

Pattern codes: **WM** Warm Minimal · **JP** Japandi · **DM** Dark Modern · **TM** Tropical Modern · **EO** Earthy Organic · **BC** Brick & Concrete · **MR** My references (proposed below).

## 3. Starting library vs. my references and this building

Climate in brief: Ahilyanagar is **hot and semi-arid**, sits in a rain shadow (moderate monsoon, about 550–650 mm a year), and is **dusty** for much of the year. At 19° N, the summer sun passes almost overhead (slightly north of overhead from late May to mid-July), and **the west face gets the harshest afternoon sun**.

Changes I made to the palettes in Section 6, and why:

1. **Base & paving darkened where it was very light** (Sand & oak, Limewash & terracotta, Charcoal brick & bronze, Stone & olive). A plinth 0–0.6 m high collects rain splash and road dust, and travertine-light colours show it within months. I kept the original as the **paving** colour and gave the **base** its own slightly darker stone:
   - Sand & oak: base `#B3A68E`
   - Limewash & terracotta: base `#A89679`
   - Charcoal brick & bronze: base `#8F887C`
   - Stone & olive: base `#ADA28F`
2. **Pure whites** (`#F2F0EB`, `#F0EEE9`) are kept, because they're in the brief, but they get a "shows dust and rain streaks" note. They are not recommended.
3. **Every wood colour means wood-look HPL, WPC or aluminium** on walls exposed to rain. Real wood is kept only for doors and soffits under cover. Nothing about the colours changes.

Recommended palette per pattern (★):

| Pattern | ★ Recommended | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 6.1 Warm Minimal | **Sand & oak** | Soft sand hides Ahilyanagar dust far better than warm white, and it is closest to the architect's cream. |
| 6.2 Japandi | **Greige & charred wood** | Greige is dust-friendly, and the charred-wood accent ties in with the dark frames in #15. |
| 6.3 Dark Modern | **White & anthracite** | Keeps the dark colour as a second surface (30%), so the main walls stay cool. A charcoal main wall on the west/south faces would heat the flats. |
| 6.4 Tropical Modern | **Sand & sandstone** | Local-looking sandstone, matches the stone piers in #5/#24, and hides dust. |
| 6.5 Earthy Organic | **Clay** | A mid-tone body ages well in dusty, sunny weather. Limewash white streaks. |
| 6.6 Brick & Concrete | **Red brick & black** | Clay brick tiles are local, cheap and weather-proof, and brick hides dirt. |

## 4. Proposed pattern: "My references", working name **Warm curves**

**Description (one line for the app):** warm cream boxes with soft rounded edges, wood underneath and in vertical strips, glass balconies and warm evening light.

**Default elements:**
- **Box frames** with rounded corners on the west bedroom windows
- One **full-height wood-look strip** on the master bedroom's blank south wall (beside the balconies)
- **Wood soffits** under every balcony and the top slab, with downlights
- Balcony fronts: **solid curved lower parapet + glass top** (as in #25)
- **Stacked-stone strip** on the stair tower base
- **Cove LED** along frame edges
- Terrace **planter edge**
- Compound wall with **LED pilasters** and a **slatted wood-look gate**

**Practical notes:** Climate: good, provided wood-look panels are used outside. Maintenance: medium (cream needs a repaint about every 5–6 years, and LED drivers need to be accessible). Cost: **$$**.
